# research · Phase 01: Competitors and Flywheel

## What's worth researching now

1. **Competitor facts.** For each competitor the student has named: what it does, for whom, how it makes money, and one size signal (revenue, users, locations, funding) with its year. Prefer filings, annual reports, and industry reports over press; company pages are `marketing` and labeled as such.

2. **Who else is in the market.** After the student has named what they know, search on the market terms they used (the person and the job) and report what you find, labeled as found by you. The student decides which make their ten. Don't search on brand names they haven't mentioned.

3. **The measure of "biggest".** The number that makes one competitor the biggest by the measure the student chose, with year and scope. If two sources disagree, report both.

4. **Evidence for the loop's arrows.** For the flywheel, each arrow is a claim: more A leads to more B. Look for evidence that it actually does for this company (for example, that selection grows traffic, or that volume lowers unit cost). Counter-evidence counts: an arrow that doesn't hold is the most useful finding.

## Frames that work at this stage

- "Who are the ten largest providers of <job> for <person>, by <measure>?"
- "What is <competitor>'s <measure> and from what year?"
- "Does <competitor>'s growth in <A> actually lead to more <B>?"
- "What has slowed <competitor>'s growth?"

## Don't

- Don't rank the competitors or say which is the right "biggest". Report numbers with their measure, year, and scope.
- Don't return a size number without saying what it measures (revenue vs. bookings vs. users) and for what year and geography.
- Don't build the flywheel. Research the arrows the student has already named.
