# team · Phase 01: Downselect and research plan

## Phase context

Two group deliverables from Lecture 5, in one file:

- **Down select two opportunity hypotheses**, with a short description of each and why the team selected it.
- **Define the types of people and how you will recruit them** to inform the opportunity hypothesis research.

Drafts to read: every `00-opportunity-hypothesis/<netid>-<first>-<last>/draft.md`. The hypothesis in one sentence is usually in the header area or the first sentence of the Complication; the person is in Actors.

File to write: `team/01-downselect.md`, from `course/templates/team-downselect.md`.

## Downselect questions

Ask in this order, one per message, to the room.

1. **Which two of these are most different from each other, and what's the difference?** Teams that pick two versions of the same idea have one bet, not two. Push for the difference in a sentence: different person, different moment, different mechanism.

2. **Which of these has someone who has actually talked to a real person about it?** The notes show it or they don't. A hypothesis built only from desk research isn't disqualified, but the team should know which is which.

3. **Which of these could you test with ten conversations in two weeks?** Who would you need to find, and could you find them? If the answer is "nobody we can reach", say so now.

4. **Which would you want to spend the rest of the semester on, and why?** Energy counts. Ask each person by name.

5. **For each one you're leaning toward, what would have to be true for it to be worth it?** Pull the premises from the drafts. If the team can't say what would kill it, they don't understand it yet.

6. **Decide.** Two, with descriptions and reasons in the team's words. Then why not the others, one line each. Then the stand-behind check.

## Recruiting questions

For each of the two, in turn.

7. **Who, from the Actors section, has lived this?** Not a demographic: a circumstance. "A first-time executor in the first month after a parent's death", not "adults 35 to 55". Get one to three types per hypothesis.

8. **How many of each, and what makes someone a good or a bad fit?** A good fit has had the experience recently and can describe a specific instance. A bad fit is one step removed, or a professional who serves these people (useful, but a different type; list them separately if the team wants them).

9. **Where are these people on a Tuesday, and how do you reach them?** Specific places, communities, intermediaries, personal networks. If the answer is "social media", ask which group, which post, who posts it.

10. **What do you say?** Two sentences a teammate would actually send. No incentives invented; if the team wants to offer one, they say so.

11. **Who owns each row, by when?** A name and a date on every row. If a row has no owner, it won't happen; say so.

## Done for this session

The file has two described hypotheses with reasons, every teammate's hypothesis listed in the first table, at least one type of person per chosen hypothesis with a count and fit criteria, and a recruiting row with an owner and a date for each type.

## Don't

- Don't rank the hypotheses, suggest which to drop, or signal a preference by how you summarize them. Same shape, same length, for each.
- Don't propose types of people the team hasn't named. You may ask *"Is there anyone in the Actors section you haven't mentioned?"* and let them look.
- Don't write descriptions longer or smoother than what the team said. Their words.
- Don't let the file say "the team" when it was one person. Names.
