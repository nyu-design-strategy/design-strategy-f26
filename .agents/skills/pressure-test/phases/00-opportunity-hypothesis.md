# pressure-test · Phase 00: Opportunity Hypothesis

## Phase context

Individual deliverable, sections: **Category · Actors · Situation · Complication · Target Outcome · Potential Solutions · Reason to Believe.** The Category is given by the professors and shared by the team.

## Checks (run all, in order)

### Removal test
Read the Complication with the Situation deleted. Does it still read the same? If yes, the two aren't actually connected: the Complication doesn't depend on anything the Situation established. Explain in one sentence which part of the Situation, if any, the Complication relies on.

### Swap test
Take any two actors. Could their descriptions be swapped without anyone noticing? If yes, the actors aren't differentiated by what they want or what they'd do. Name the pair.

### Claim/source table
List every factual or quantitative claim in the draft. For each, give the source the draft points to (or the `sources/` page that matches) and one status:

- `supported`: the source says this, at this scope
- `source doesn't say this`: the source exists but doesn't make this claim
- `no source`: the claim has none
- `stale`: the data is old enough that "now" is doubtful (roughly 2020 or earlier for market or behavior claims)
- `scope mismatch`: different geography, population, or segment than the claim

Output as a table. Don't skip claims that sound like common knowledge.

### "Any-brief" test
Quote up to two sentences from the draft that would be equally true in any domain or industry ("customers value convenience", "trust is essential"). For each, ask what is specific to this situation that the sentence is standing in for.

### Outcome test
Ask: *"In 12 months, how would you know whether this worked?"* Does the Target Outcome give a way to tell? If the draft names an outcome but not how you'd observe it, flag that. If it names a solution instead of an outcome, flag that too.

### Solution spread
Place each proposed solution in one bucket:

- **minimal**: the smallest thing that would help the person described
- **ambitious**: a full product or service
- **lateral**: changes who does what, who pays, or which actor is a partner, rather than adding a thing

If all the solutions land in the same bucket, or are the same kind (all apps, all consumer-paid, all marketplaces), ask: *"What's a version with no software? One where someone else pays? One where the actor who benefits from the status quo becomes a partner?"*

## Don't

- Don't propose solutions of your own, in any bucket.
- Don't rewrite claims to make them supportable; only report the status.
- Don't comment on writing style or length unless it hides a structural problem.
