# tensions · Phase 02: Differentiation

## Phase context

Lecture 6 in-class exercise (60 minutes, Miro): craft possible ways to differentiate; identify tensions across the four fits (market, model, product, channel); plot tensions as 2x2 axes; place competitors, substitutes, incumbents and new entrants to find white space; iterate.

Homework (group, 5%): two matrix boards showing how the two carried-forward hypotheses are different, with two or three sentences each. That goes in `team/02-differentiation.md` via `/team` when the phase file for it ships; this skill produces the material for it.

## Inputs in the repo

- `team/01-downselect.md`: the two hypotheses, their actors, the recruiting plan.
- `01-competitors-flywheel/<student>/draft.md`: each teammate's ten competitors with what/for whom/money/size/direct-indirect, the market definition, the biggest one and its flywheel.
- `sources/`: the size signals, if a placement needs a number.

## Notes for this phase

- A flywheel from Phase 01 is a tension waiting to happen: the thing the biggest competitor gets more of as it grows is one pole; the opposite is the other. Point this out once if nobody raises it.
- "Direct" competitors from the tables usually cluster in one quadrant. The substitutes and "doing nothing" are what stretch the axes. Make sure they're on the map.
- The two hypotheses need two different boards. If the team wants to reuse axes across both, ask whether the same tension really applies to both persons.
