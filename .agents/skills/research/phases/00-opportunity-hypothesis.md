# research · Phase 00: Opportunity Hypothesis

## What's worth researching now

The Opportunity Hypothesis needs evidence for three things, and research should aim at whichever the student's notes are thinnest on:

1. **The Situation is real.** The context the student describes exists at the scale they say. Market size numbers live here, but so do behavior data and history.
2. **The Complication is happening.** Something changed or is changing. Regulation, price, technology, demographics. This is where "what changed?" from office-hours gets its evidence.
3. **The landscape.** What already exists for the person described: companies, workarounds, public programs. Search on category terms, not brand names the student hasn't mentioned. Don't hint at "obvious" players; find what the search finds.

The Reason to Believe section is built from these. The Target Outcome needs a way to measure it; if the student has one, look for a baseline number.

## Frames that work at this stage

- "Who, specifically, has this problem, and how many of them are there?"
- "What do they do about it today?" (workarounds, incumbents, doing nothing)
- "What changed in the last five years?"
- "Who makes money from it staying this way?"

## Don't

- Don't research solutions or competitors to a solution the student hasn't settled on. Research the problem.
- Don't return a market-size number without saying what population and year it covers.
